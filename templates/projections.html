A quick primer

Coordinates need
a frame of reference.

A coordinate reference system tells us where coordinates sit on Earth. A map projection then transforms that curved surface into a flat plane.

01

Geographic CRS

Uses latitude and longitude on a model of the Earth. EPSG:4326 is a familiar example, but degrees are not a consistent unit of ground distance.

53.3498° N, 6.2603° W
02

Projected CRS

Transforms coordinates to a plane, usually in metres. A local CRS such as UTM zone 29N is suitable for many measurements around Dublin.

EPSG:32629 · metres
03

Fit for purpose

There is no universally best projection. Match the CRS to the place, extent, and property that the analysis or map needs to preserve.

Question → place → property

The key takeaway

“Which projection should I use?”
always starts with “What do you need to preserve?”
